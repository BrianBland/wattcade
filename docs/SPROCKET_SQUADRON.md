# Sprocket Squadron

Sprocket Squadron retains its name, `starfighter` ID, and profile/history association.

## Intent

Hold the groove, command the squadron. An bright arcade science-fiction shooter flying across carriers above the clouds powered by endurance riding through a continuous music selection. Automatic movement, aiming, firing, and defensive interception keep hands free. Optional periodic manual dodges are a later experiment. Cadenza remains the interval/steering game.

Music is central; song-by-song preparation should eventually be optional. Reuse the shared picker, mix engine, heard-audio clock, and timeline. Built-in generated soundtracks already work through the picker. Missing analysis produces steady procedural encounters, never invented mandatory rhythm challenges. A separate podcast/no-music mode is deferred.

## Riding-style craft

Craft are actual riding contracts, selected before the music starts. Do not switch trainer modes mid-ride. Show mode, effort, and unlock conditions clearly; auto-launch the remembered available craft after 20 seconds of pedalling.

| Craft | Contract | Combat identity | Availability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cadence Cruiser | Rider controls power with virtual gears; 56–75% FTP steady Zone 2. Music changes battle intensity, not the endurance band. No reward for riding above the ceiling. | Offensive: pulse fire and homing lock-on volleys; filling every lock slot fires a full-lock volley (×1.5 damage, bonus score). Nova clears ordinary enemies. | Starter (the only craft for a new rider) |
| Resonance Escort | ERG around 65% FTP; restrained section changes within Zone 2, maximum about 70%. Grace for target settling; maintain cadence and measured power. | Defensive: two wingmen shoot down nearby bullets for score; a wide beam sweeps the ship's lane on each phrase; fewer, slower locks. Nova is a shield burst that eats every bullet and staggers enemies. | Cruiser level 3, or converted legacy credit |
| Future craft | Cadence-focused, low-cadence endurance, workout-following, or variable-effort contracts. Explicitly opt into their demands. | Distinct weapon/defense mechanics, not permanent damage superiority. | Later |

ERG availability is checked through the shared context before selecting a craft or issuing ERG commands. Disable only the Escort on an unsupported trainer; the Cruiser remains available in SIM mode. Both hoods' shifters adjust Cruiser gears. The analog paddles (a firm squeeze, re-armed on release), arrow keys, and d-pad cycle the selected formation left/right (flagship batteries group with their flagship). B releases painted locks on the next beat. Z or the left power button snaps to an elite wing, else the flagship. Locks fill the selected formation first, then spill to the nearest ordinary enemies. Selection sticks until that formation is gone. Auto-selection picks the flagship, else the nearest ordinary formation, and never picks an elite wing. A/Y, the right auxiliary button, or touch activates Nova. D-pad left/right and touch arrows also retarget. Already painted locks stay committed; the next acquisition starts with the chosen target.

## Effort and threat

- Use smoothed measured power and cadence, not individual noisy samples. Startup grace and a few seconds of shortfall tolerance. ERG changes settle gradually; target information reflects the command requested by the game. Shared mixer difficulty/safety remain authoritative.
- On target: guns fire and the ship flies a choreographed route, scheduled seconds ahead: it holds a lane, then glides to the next, leaving and landing on beats. When a phrase boundary is imminent, it holds and launches a long sweep across the field exactly on the phrase. Enemy fire is authored against that route, so an on-target ship is never hit: shots graze past or leave a gate it flies through. No reactive dodging, no jitter. Sustained under-target effort reduces firing and makes the pilot sloppy: the ship lags its route and fire is no longer authored to miss, so bullets hit the shield; living enemies ram the ship (and burst). On target, an enemy that gets past counts as escaped the moment it crosses the ship's row, but keeps flying: fighters peel away around the ship and leave the bottom of the screen, and ground units scroll off. Nothing vanishes beside the ship. Above Zone 2 is also off-contract, but adds no threat pressure: withhold charge/progression and prompt Ease off.
- Hands stay free for targeting, not dodging: right paddles choose which enemies the volleys hit, so the rider influences how much of each formation dies even while invulnerable.
- Recovery is legitimate play. A Nova tap clears ordinary enemies, damages a boss, and grants 20 seconds of escort cover at 50% FTP in ERG (easy spin in SIM). No target penalties, with guns active while pedalling. One button; no holding/repeated tapping. Charge comes from on-target time; ability cannot perpetually recharge itself during cover.
- Hull failure causes a brief protected recovery and rejoin, not game over. Music continues. Gun growth is retained. A music set ending ends the ride cleanly; procedural headless fallback can be endless.
- Default Zone 2 cruiser has no compulsory surges. ERG escort uses announced, bounded section changes. Never use BPM as instantaneous wattage or change resistance per beat.

## Music director

- Sections: intro/verse = cruise formations, build = armored arrivals, drop = swarms and broad volleys, break/outro = lighter encounters and cover. Approximate functional labels, not guaranteed musical form.
- Phrases: change formation/pattern and schedule eligible boss entrances. Beats/onsets: firing rhythm, entrances, impacts, scenery pulses. Keep density bounded, including long dense EDM passages.
- Do not iterate/replay historical events after timeline revisions. Preserve announced targets. The first build freezes a short lookahead announcement locally; richer shared confidence/commit contracts are delegated to #64.
- Cadence challenges are optional bonuses only, roughly a phrase long and spaced minutes apart. Use trustworthy rhythm and a comfortable band; candidate BPM ratios 1, 1/2, 3/4, 2/3. Prefer near 90 RPM within 70–100 RPM, otherwise skip. Never judge exact crank phase from ordinary cadence telemetry. Until local confidence exists, enable only full-analysis timelines with a consistent beat grid.
- First boss eligible at about 2½ minutes, then every 6 minutes; announce 8 seconds ahead, align to a phrase if possible, use bounded timing fallback for nonrhythmic music. Fight 45–90 seconds, then escape and continue if not defeated. Bosses do not raise workout intensity. Three individually lockable batteries protect the main hull. Destroying batteries damages the flagship and exposes its reactor; survivors leave with an escaping flagship. Different silhouettes and attack patterns cycle; music controls their volleys/vulnerability feel.

## Progression

Within a ride: arsenal tiers unlock with on-target time, at 0/2/6/12/20/30/40/50 minutes for tiers 1–8. The tier is capped by the craft's level ceiling: 5 for a new craft, rising to 8. Each tier paints locks faster (`tierLockScale`^(tier−1)), adds a lock slot and laser damage, and visibly unfolds pods. Better weapons genuinely clear more; HP does not scale to cancel them. Steady-ride clear rate for a new Cruiser runs roughly 55% at tier 2 to 90%+ at tier 5. Levelled craft reach full clears earlier. Formations have 6–9 fighters (5 in calm sections). Fully cleared air formations of 5+ are PERFECT, with a growing streak bonus; the big callout appears only at the first perfect and at streak milestones. Auto-collect all rewards; no shop interruptions.

Across rides, each craft levels from its own on-target minutes (levels 2–10 at 45/90/150/210/285/360/450/540/630 minutes). Levels never add raw damage. Cruiser rewards: wingman, arsenal 6 + Escort unlock, quick lock, second wingman, arsenal 7, heavy escort (homing missile from behind the ship), arsenal 8, quick lock II, ace finish. Escort rewards: wider interception, arsenal 6, wider beam, third wingman, arsenal 7, heavy escort, arsenal 8, wider beam II, ace finish. Levels can rise mid-ride and apply at once. The hangar shows each craft's level, progress, and next reward. Save v3 keeps `craftS` (seconds per craft) and a sticky `unlocked` list; early v3 saves seed the Cruiser with all groove time. The old best score, the legacy payload, and the one-time conversion of investment credit are preserved. The internal ID, achievement IDs, and lifetime counters remain stable.

Playtesting: the tunable `starfighter.testerUnlockAll = 1` (debug overlay → Tuning, or `?tester=1` on the dev page) unlocks every craft at level 10; `starfighter.arsenalPace` speeds up in-ride tiers. An arcade-wide tester mode would be a shell feature.

## Presentation

Three.js perspective camera with angled forward motion, modeled white/cyan craft with sprocket engine housings, magenta/orange enemy formations, a continuous route from carrier runways above blue clouds through a waterfront city to canyon waterways. The terrain scrolls at `GROUND_SPEED` (22 units/s, about 4 ship lengths a second); ground units ride it at exactly that speed and ground explosions/smoke stay put on it. Scale anchors make speed and altitude legible: jets parked on the carrier deck share the player's silhouette, runway edge lights are regularly spaced, city traffic shows head/taillights, boats on the canyon river leave wakes, and cloud wisps pass below the ship over the open cloud sea. Biomes are generated per segment from its seed (render/terrain.ts + render/space.ts), and the next one cross-fades in at the point where the ship will be when the segment changes, under the scorecard. Carrier: varied island towers, parked-jet rows, cranes. City: a seeded street grid (shared by shader and instancing), block-by-block buildings, low-rise under the play lane, a varied skyline beyond, parks, plazas, rooftop antennas, an optional elevated highway with traffic, and harbour water. Canyon: a two-frequency winding river of varying width with rapids, banked rock clusters, mesas, arches, and outposts. Ocean fleet: deep water with whitecaps; carriers, battleships, and destroyers under way with wakes; ground units ride barges. Movers are world-anchored, so they wrap out of view. Spatial tiles carry tall layered buildings, rooftop antennas, hangars, bridges, rocky cliffs, and outposts; scenery streams beyond the camera with fixed-size instance pools. Destroyable ground batteries and tracked tanks scroll beneath aircraft and independently aim at the player. Enemy bullets are shaped, camera-facing sprites (render/bullets.ts, one instanced draw). Pink shots are orbs with a hot core, a coloured ring and a halo; amber shots are needles aligned with their flight; wall shots are diamonds. Each bullet has a fading trail, and rings brighten on the beat. Half-resolution bloom on bright additive effects (it disables itself after sustained slow frames), curved additive laser trails, yellow locks with smaller rings across the selected formation and brackets on its lead, larger wall bullets, bullets stretched along their flight, evasion afterimages, pink/amber projectile glows, ship shadows, rising explosion smoke, larger flagship blasts, bright impact cores, tumbling debris, and brief shockwaves supply combat feedback. Fixed-size instance pools and recycled scenery bound graphics resource use. Different craft silhouettes, wingmen, and weapon tiers must be visible. Music remains clear; avoid punitive audio degradation or repeated loud shooting effects. Restrained beat pulses; no strobe requirement.

HUD: there is no title during play; an arsenal chip sits top-left and a compact, legible glass stats panel top-right (score, clear %, locks/capacity with pips). It floats over a full-bleed scene in the shared arcade style (main.css tokens, `.card`/`.btn`/`.tag`, neon italic numerals). It shows craft/arsenal, score, ride clear rate, lock pips, effort warnings, boss bar/arrival, shield, song/section, Nova, and big callouts for banners and PERFECT formations. Watts, zone, and controller hints belong to the shell HUD and are not duplicated. All play focus and interactive UI above actual shell insets, portrait and landscape. Touch has previous/next target arrows and the same Nova action and pre-ride craft selection as controller. No implementation terminology in player prompts.

## Segments, bosses, and scorecards

The ride is a sequence of places. Normally each song is one segment. A track longer than 7 minutes (a DJ mix) splits roughly every 5½ minutes at a natural seam: a break or build start, else a phrase. Songs under 2 minutes merge with the next. Without a timeline, segments are fixed `bossEveryS` windows. The current segment is committed and never shifts when the timeline is revised; the next is planned when it begins. Each segment has a seed (stable per song) and a biome that never repeats its neighbour's. It has at most one flagship, which arrives on the strongest drop in the second half and leaves before the breather. In the last 14 seconds no new enemies arrive; in the last 6, a scorecard shows the letter grade (S/A/B/C/D) with clear %, on-target %, perfect wings, elites, and the flagship result. The grade weighs steady riding 45, clear rate 30, flair 15 (half perfect wings, half elites) and flagship 10. A rider who never touches the paddles tops out at A; S needs attention. Watts above the target never count.

## Amp

Following Beat Hazard, weapons swell with the music: laser damage, pulse damage, fire rate, and laser width scale with smoothed intensity, relative to the track's level over roughly the last minute (so any genre works, and average power is unchanged). Enemy density already follows intensity. A small Amp meter sits under the arsenal chip.

## Beat weave

On target, the ship sways around its planned lane with swing peaks on beats (sim/weave.ts). It sways every 2 beats in verses and builds, every beat in drops, and once a bar in calm sections. Amplitude follows intensity, capped at 30 u/s peak sway speed. Each phrase picks a pattern from its section's set, never repeating the previous phrase's: sway, figure-eight (a vertical bob at double speed), beat dip (half-speed sway, a sharp dip on each beat), or slow sweep (calm sections). Patterns cross-fade over the last two beats of a phrase; sections and tempos cross-fade too. The weave is part of the route, so fire is authored to miss it. Lane changes are rarer and start and end on swing peaks, during which the sway fades out. Occasionally (a drop's phrase start, at most every 35 s) the ship barrel-rolls over two beats, with wingmen following a beat apart. Below target the flight goes sloppy: the sway runs late and smaller, a lazy wobble creeps in, and a loose spring overshoots, so bullets connect. Tunable `starfighter.weave` (0 = lane changes only).

## Route and turns

The ship follows a world route (sim/route.ts): straight legs joined by constant-curvature arcs of 30–70° (at most ~4.4°/s). Turns happen only over open biomes (city, ocean), start on phrases, and never fall within ~25 s of a segment change. Carrier and canyon stretches stay straight, and the next biome's frame starts at the switch pose. Combat (air enemies, bullets, beams, choreography) lives in the flight frame, so readability never changes in a turn. The world (ground shader, scenery, ground units) is placed through the route pose: ground units are world-anchored and swing beneath the ship. The city and ocean are laid out on world axes in every direction; blocks within ~42 units of the route ahead stay low-rise, and the fleet keeps clear of the path. Movers sit on continuous indexed lanes, so none wraps in view. The camera rolls gently into turns.

## Targeting and elites

About once a minute, outside calm sections and flagship fights, an elite wing of five gold fighters with breathing gold rings crosses the far field from one flank and leaves. A HUD hint (◀ / ▶ Elite wing) points the paddle direction. Elites are only targeted when the rider selects them. Each elite kill scores more than an ordinary one; a full clear adds a bonus, 25 Nova charge, and a banner. Ignored elites never count against the clear rate. Chasing one protects the PERFECT streak for a few seconds. Measured: an attentive bot clears ~40% of elites and scores ~19% more over 30 minutes than a rider who never touches the paddles.

## Visible growth

In a ride, each arsenal tier adds a distinct part that unfolds with a slight overshoot on a bar line. Tier 2 adds twin nose guns, 3 spread cannons, 4 prism wing-tip emitters, 5 extended wing fins, 6 afterburner ribbons that breathe on the beat, 7 glowing panel lines, and 8 a spine lance with a halo. Lasers widen with the tier and move from cyan through white-cyan to a gold core. Full-lock volleys burn gold and ring the ship; impact bursts grow with the tier. Pulse shots go from dots to bolts to long spears, gold at the top tiers.

Across rides, craft levels show as livery. Level 3 adds wing and spine trim in a light tint of the rider colour, level 5 pod stripes, level 7 rider-tinted engines and afterburners, and level 10 gold trim and gold accents. Wingmen fly in the rider's colours, and the heavy escort is its own twin-boom gunship. The hangar shows the selected craft large and turning on the free side of the screen: it wears its livery and the arsenal it grows into each ride, with companions alongside. Each card shows the level, its progress, the next reward, and the minutes remaining.

## Musical layer (subtle, cumulative)

Lancers sit on the flanks of gunner and armour formations. A dashed pink line shows each one's lane about a bar ahead, and it fires an instant beam on the bar line; a second lancer takes the next beat, which forms gates. On target, a beam that would cross the route is held back, so beams graze past; under target they fire regardless. Killing a lancer during its warning cancels the beam. Every bar line also sends a light wave from the horizon toward the camera: runway edge lights chase, window strips brighten in turn, and water ripples. Enemy running lights breathe on the beat. A slow colour grade follows the song section: builds warm, drops brighten and saturate, breaks cool and dim. No flashing.


Locks paint on the sixteenth-note grid, so the rising ticks play as a rhythm. Volleys release on a bar line (a beat if late), and every laser in a volley lands together on the next beat. Enemies fire on beats; route moves leave and land on beats and sweep on phrases. Bullets, enemies, runway dashes, window lights, the camera, and bloom carry small beat/bar pulses. No single effect is loud; together they lock the picture to the track. Measured: 100% of lock ticks on sixteenths, 99% of volleys on beats (56% on bar lines), 100% of impacts on beats.

## Lock-on combat slice

Continuous pulse guns support a repeated anticipation/payoff cycle. Holding the riding target paints one lock about every 0.38 seconds, starting with the priority target and spreading to other visible enemies. Extra locks can stack on the priority enemy. Automatic volleys release on a beat/phrase after about 2.4 seconds, with a 3.4-second fallback for sparse analysis. Curved lasers follow committed targets and deliver impact damage after travel time. No trigger tapping or extra wattage is required. Lock ticks ascend quietly and volleys add a restrained whoosh through the shared sound graph/mute setting.

Phrase-driven V formations, staggered arrivals, and sweeping rows enter continuously from well beyond the view ahead or from either flank. Flagships also approach from beyond the horizon, with attached batteries following their hull. Enemies outside the combat area cannot fire or acquire locks. Air formations have up to thirteen fighters, with separate ground groups of tanks and batteries. Moderate, readable density (RayStorm, not bullet hell; roughly 30–80 projectiles on screen): only designated gunners in each formation fire, volleys wait for a beat, and patterns are aimed singles, three-shot fans, short tank bursts, and a 13-shot rotating flagship fan with petals at slow speeds. Each on-target pattern is rotated slightly so it grazes the route, or the connecting shots are dropped to open a gate. Gunners show a closing muzzle-charge ring before firing. In builds and drops, a gunner can fire a 13-shot amber wall on a downbeat with a wide gate where the route will be. Near misses spark, tick quietly, and score. Bullet cores and glows pulse subtly with each beat. Projectile and enemy budgets remain bounded; steady endurance earns protection without additional wattage. The first flagship provides a concrete target-selection payoff in the opening few minutes; the same experience extends through the full mix.
